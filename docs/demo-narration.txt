Fraud alerts are not verdicts. They are invitations to investigate. GraphSentinel is our submission for the TigerGraph by Hacker House Goa 2026 agentic fraud investigation challenge. It combines graph traversal, institutional case memory, calibrated reasoning, and a deterministic policy engine so that every recommendation has an evidence trail.

The benchmark contains more than five hundred ninety thousand transactions, one hundred forty four thousand identity records, five thousand five hundred sixty five closed cases, and twenty scored investigations. Our graph models customers, cards, transactions, composite device profiles, email domains, billing regions, closed cases, and new investigation cases. Relationships capture ownership, card activity, device use, geography, sequence, and prior-case connections.

The workflow has four stages. First, a risk score, customer report, or analyst request opens an investigation. Second, bounded GSQL traversals gather the account baseline, transaction window, shared devices, and region neighbors. Third, Graph RAG retrieves comparable closed cases as institutional memory. Finally, a deterministic implementation of rules R one through R ten chooses allowed actions and exact approval routes. The language model can summarize evidence, but it cannot invent identifiers or override policy.

Here is case H H G zero fourteen. The incoming model score is only zero point zero five, which would look harmless in a score-only system. TigerGraph reveals something very different. The exact Samsung mobile fingerprint appears across many customers and connects to four confirmed historical fraud cases. GraphSentinel labels this coordinated behavior as an undocumented shared-device ring instead of forcing it into a familiar category. The final actions recommend blocking the affected card through L one, creating an internal case automatically, monitoring connected cards, and routing the suspicious activity report to L two. Every prior case and connected card shown here exists in the supplied dataset.

Now look at case H H G zero zero six. Four online purchases land inside thirty minutes with near-equal amounts, creating one thousand nine hundred six dollars and seven cents of exposure. This is not card testing under the supplied policy, because the authorizations are already high value rather than small probes. The agent records a rapid high-value burst as an undocumented pattern. The customer denial settles the verdict, the card block routes to L one, and the exposure threshold triggers a report.

Restraint matters just as much as detection. Case H H G zero ten enters with a zero point nine model score and a one thousand dollar online purchase. That score is still only one signal. The agent requests verification and explicitly records the simulated response allowed by the benchmark. When the customer confirms the merchant, amount, and channel, rule R three closes the case as legitimate. Exposure becomes zero, no suspicious activity report is created, and no card is blocked.

The final answer pack is balanced: ten fraud outcomes and ten legitimate outcomes. All twenty JSON files follow the exact required schema. A validator checks required fields, enums, approval routes, legitimate-case invariants, and agreement between report decisions and final actions. A second audit resolves every transaction, card, and prior-case identifier against the raw benchmark and recalculates exposure from source amounts.

Resolved investigations can be written back into TigerGraph through the write investigation case query, making each outcome reusable evidence for the next analyst. The repository includes the schema, loading jobs, core GSQL queries, REST client, deterministic generator, tests, analyst console, architecture, and full runbook.

GraphSentinel’s principle is simple: graph first, policy last, and evidence throughout. The result is an agent that knows when to investigate deeper, when to protect the customer, and just as importantly, when to stop.
